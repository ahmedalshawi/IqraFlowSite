SYNCHRONISED KHATMS · COMING SOON

One pace.
Everyone finishes.

In a shared khatm the group splits one Quran. In a synchronised khatm, everyone reads the WHOLE Quran - to one shared schedule. The creator sets the pace ("20 verses a day", "a juz a week", "starting Monday"), and every reader gets a reminder telling them exactly what to read to stay with the group.

1
Set the pace - pages or verses, every day, every three days, every week.
2
Read your range - miss a few days and the range simply grows until you are caught up; read ahead and it tells you when to come back.
3
Private by design - join with a code, the group sees only a member count, and leaving is silent.

QUESTIONS

Before you ask

What is IqraFlow?
IqraFlow is a free Quran reading app. It is live on the web at app.iqraflow.app, with iOS and Android apps to follow. It shows the Quran in the Madinah mushaf script with tajweed colouring and word-by-word translation, records how much you read each day, keeps a streak, and lets a group finish the Quran together in shared khatms. It needs an account so that your reading position, bookmarks, statistics and khatms follow you from one device to the next - created with an email address and password, with Sign in with Google, or with Sign in with Apple. Full detail is in the privacy policy and the terms of use; questions go to support@iqraflow.app.
What does IqraFlow ask of your Google or Apple account?

Choosing Sign in with Google asks Google only for the basic sign-in scopes - openid, userinfo.email and userinfo.profile. Between them they tell IqraFlow your account identifier, your email address and your name. The identifier links you to your own reading data, the email address is how we reach you about your account and how you recover it, and your name is what the other readers in a shared khatm see beside the part you have taken.

Sign in with Apple is the same idea with Apple's controls: it shares your name and email address, and Apple lets you hide your real address behind a private relay - IqraFlow works exactly the same either way.

IqraFlow asks for nothing else. No access to Gmail, Drive, Contacts, Calendar or any other Google or Apple service, and it never posts anything on your behalf. Your reading data is never sold and never used for advertising - there are no ads and no third-party advertising or analytics SDKs in the app - and you can delete your account, and everything in it, from Settings at any time. Full detail: privacy policy.

Is IqraFlow free?
IqraFlow is free for everyone - no ads, no subscriptions, nothing locked away. We don't want your money: all we ask is that you remember us in your duas and spread the word. Our hope is that as many people as possible build a consistent connection with the Quran - and complete it together through shared khatms.
Does it really work offline?
In the iOS and Android apps, yes: the entire Quran text, its bundled translations and the word-by-word meanings ship inside the app, so reading works with no connection at all, and recitation audio can be downloaded over Wi-Fi then played anywhere. The web app at app.iqraflow.app needs a connection - it is a browser page. Your progress syncs to your account whenever you are online, so it follows you to every device you sign in on.
What is a grace token?
Life happens. Miss a day and a grace token is spent automatically to protect your streak - you earn them back by reading consistently. It keeps the habit merciful instead of punishing.
How do shared khatms work?
Create a khatm, share its short invite code, and each person claims a portion - by juz or surah. Progress syncs live for the group while personal reading stays on your device. No social feed, no follower counts.
Which script, translations and reciters are included?
The Uthmani Hafs script of the Madinah mushaf with colour-coded tajweed, an IndoPak Nastaleeq option, and a choice of reading faces - KFGQPC Hafs, Scheherazade New, Amiri Quran and Lateef. Saheeh International is built in, with more than 125 translations available in many languages and word-by-word meanings in five. Fifteen reciters are included, among them Nasser Al-Qatami, Maher Al-Muaiqly, Abdul Basit and Al-Husary.
When does it launch, and on what?
The web app is live now at app.iqraflow.app - free, in your browser, nothing to install. The iOS and Android apps are in store review preparation.
Does my progress follow me across devices?
Yes - that is what the account is for. Reading position, bookmarks, streaks, hasanat, statistics and khatms all sync to your account, so you can read on your phone in the morning and pick up the same verse in the browser at lunch. The app is built offline-first: reading is recorded on the device first and synced when a connection is available, so nothing is lost in between.
